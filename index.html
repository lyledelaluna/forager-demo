Supplier documents, from inbox to filed record.

A QA manager's inbox fills with supplier certificates. Forager reads each one, checks it against your checklist, and files it only after a person approves.

When an auditor asks who accepted a document and why, there's rarely an answer. Forager records the decision and the reason. You'll use the real app, with fictional suppliers and sample documents.

Three separate authorities, on purpose. No single one is trusted to wave through a real compliance gap.LLM — the AI model that reads documents.

01

The LLM reads.

It filters out junk mail, classifies each attachment, extracts the fields, and reports what it found against your checklist.

02

The code judges.

A fixed rule turns those findings into Pass, Advisory, or Issues, using severities you set. The model never grades itself.

03

The human decides.

Nothing is filed until a person approves it. Overriding a failed check takes a written reason, and it's logged.

The problem

Supplier paperwork is chased by hand.

Certificates, statements, and specs arrive by email in whatever format each supplier prefers. Someone has to open every one, identify it, and decide whether it's complete.

Errors slip through

Not from carelessness. From volume, and no time to check every field.

Records fragment

Company and ingredient names drift in spelling from one email to the next.

Review is slow

One document at a time, and expiry dates live in someone's memory.

No record of the call

Who approved what, and on what grounds, usually lives in someone's inbox.

The pipeline

Seven stages, one human gate.

Every document takes the same path before it's treated as an accepted record.
